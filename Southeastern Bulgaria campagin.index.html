01 — Main Issues

What's going wrong

Four interconnected problems — rooted in geography, economics, and infrastructure — that have been driving people out of Burgas, Sliven, Stara Zagora, and Yambol for decades.

Population
–12%

125,000 people left in 10 years

Young people relocate to Sofia or Western Europe for better opportunities. Yambol's replacement rate has dropped to just 60.1% — meaning the population is shrinking faster than it can replace itself.

Geographic factor

The region's peripheral location — far from Sofia and near the Turkish and Greek borders — limits economic connections. Rural settlements lack the density to attract services, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of decline.

Energy
Coal

Maritsa East is running out of time

The Maritsa East basin is Bulgaria's largest energy source and one of the EU's biggest lignite coal operations. Thousands of local jobs depend on it — but EU climate policy will end coal power by the 2030s.

Geographic factor

The basin sits on the Thracian Plain in Stara Zagora province — a flat, open landscape ideal for solar farms. The region receives some of the highest annual sunshine hours in Bulgaria, making it a natural candidate for renewable energy.

Employment
46pt

Education shapes who can find work here

Among people with a university degree, 83% are employed in the region. Among those with only primary education, the rate drops to 37%. A 46-percentage-point gap that reflects who the local economy is and isn't working for.

Geographic factor

Universities are concentrated in Burgas and Stara Zagora. Smaller towns and villages have little access to vocational training or higher education, locking rural residents into low-wage or no-wage situations.

Rural Access
Rural

Villages cut off from the modern economy

Dozens of rural settlements in Yambol and Sliven have no reliable broadband, poor road connections, and no local employers. Without digital or physical infrastructure, remote work and new businesses are impossible to start.

Geographic factor

The Balkan mountain range creates a natural barrier between the northern plains and the southern coast, making road and rail investment more complex — but also more impactful when done well.

How these issues affect different groups

The same regional problems hit workers, young people, and rural families in different ways. Here is how each audience experiences these challenges:

⚡ Coal workers

Job security is disappearing

Workers at Maritsa East face an uncertain future. Without a planned transition, thousands risk unemployment when coal operations wind down — with no retraining and no alternative employer nearby.

🎓 Young people

There is no reason to stay

Graduates and school leavers see few jobs, no affordable housing, and poor transport. Leaving for Sofia or abroad is not a preference — it often feels like the only option.

🚆 Rural communities

Cut off from services and work

Villages with no bus, no broadband, and no nearby hospital are functionally isolated. The regional decline starts here — and recovery has to start here too.

02 — The Plan

Three pillars,
one region

The development plan is built around three investment areas. Each pillar targets a specific geographic and economic problem — and a specific group of people. Click each one to read more.

01
Green Energy

Transform Maritsa East

The Thracian Plain — where Maritsa East sits — receives over 2,400 hours of sunlight per year, making it one of the best locations in Europe for solar generation. The plan invests in solar parks and wind facilities to replace coal-based electricity production. Every affected coal worker is offered a funded retraining programme in renewable engineering or related technical fields.

Solar parks Wind energy Worker retraining
Audience: Coal workers and Stara Zagora communities

This pillar directly addresses the fear of job loss. Retraining is not optional or vague — it is a funded, structured programme workers can plan around.

02
Infrastructure

Connect every community

The Southeastern Region has four provinces but no fast rail link between them. The plan upgrades railway lines across Burgas, Sliven, Stara Zagora, and Yambol, expands the Port of Burgas to increase trade capacity, and delivers broadband and road improvements to rural municipalities currently cut off from the regional economy.

Railway upgrades Port of Burgas Rural broadband
Audience: Rural communities and employers

Better infrastructure means rural residents can reach jobs and services, and employers can consider locations outside city centres — spreading economic activity more fairly across the region.

03
Education and Retention

Give young people a reason to stay

University-business partnerships create direct pathways from study to local employment. Affordable housing support targets graduates who might otherwise leave for Sofia. Startup hubs in Burgas and Stara Zagora provide workspace, mentoring, and small grants for new businesses — building a local economy that young people want to be part of.

Startup hubs Affordable housing Vocational training
Audience: Young people aged 18–30

This pillar speaks directly to the decision to stay or go. Housing and jobs are the two main reasons young people leave — this pillar addresses both.

03 — Opinion
May 2026

Our region is shrinking.
It doesn't have to.

District Governor
Regional Development Council
Southeastern Level 2 Region

Between 2011 and 2021, the Southeastern Region lost 125,000 people — roughly the population of Stara Zagora city. Young people are leaving for Sofia, for Germany, for anywhere with a visible future. That is not a natural trend. It is the predictable outcome of decades of underinvestment in a region that was treated as peripheral.

"The Maritsa East coal jobs will change whether we plan for it or not. We are choosing to plan — and to plan with the workers, not around them."

Geography shapes this story. The Thracian Plain gives us exceptional solar potential. The Balkan mountain range made infrastructure hard to build — but that is not a reason to stop trying. The Port of Burgas sits on the Black Sea coast, connecting us to trade routes most of Bulgaria cannot access. These are advantages we have not fully used.

Geographic context

The Southeast is a Level 2 statistical region under EU classification — meaning it qualifies for cohesion funding, but is currently too poor to maximise those opportunities. Raising economic output to Level 1 status would unlock significantly higher EU development grants for the region and for Bulgaria as a whole.

The plan asks for €700M to €1B over several years — drawn from EU structural funds, the Bulgarian state budget, and private sector partnerships. That is not an abstract figure. It is solar panels on the Thracian Plain. It is a train that actually connects Yambol to Burgas. It is a startup grant for a graduate who wanted to stay but needed one reason to.

Three pillars. Four provinces. One region that deserves to stop being left behind.
